\textbf{Deployment.} EchoScribe runs on the clinician's machine; only the configured model vendors receive audio or text, so an institution can restrict processing to vendors its governance policy approves. Sentence-level flags give a reviewer a concrete place to look instead of an unexplained confidence score~\cite{guo2017calibration}.

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) ACI-Bench and PriMock57 are medical, and AnnoMI has no reference notes; no public corpus of counselling or mentoring sessions with reference notes exists\ifmentoring, and the mentoring sessions we ran are acted and share personas\else, so GROW notes were not evaluated\fi. (ii) Support labels come from a model; the planted-error test bounds how often it misses errors of three kinds, but subtler omissions and misattributions need human review~\cite{asgari2025}. (iii) Simulated and mock speech has no overlap or strong emotion, and at 0~dB our attribution is not better than cloud diarization. (iv) Results depend on model versions that vendors retire. (v) The risk screen has not been validated against clinician labels. Blinded rating kits for notes and verifier agreement are released with the code. \textbf{Ethics:} no patient or clinical data were used; experiments use public datasets, simulated speech and scripted role-plays recorded by volunteers who knew the audio would be processed by the system, and free-tier model vendors may use submitted text for training, so deployments with real sessions should use institutionally approved vendors or local models.
